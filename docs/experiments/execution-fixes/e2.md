- E1's five depth-ratchet runs;

- gate_right_swing for F2, the failing seeds 4, 7, 11 and 19 and the passing seeds 1, 2, 3 and 5.

Each run used its baseline's B5 config. Each reproduces its original, from `experiment_runs/e1/{ratchet,abs,absinf}`,
frame by frame: State and Event frames are identical apart from the diagnostics keys (`model_rollout.py same-run`,

measurement describes the very runs E1 analysed.

Beside them:

- an open-loop excitation through `plant_replay`, with no B5: 29 segments (seven speed steps, nine yaw steps, four

- the arm feedforward check (T8).

The excitation's steps run from 0.002 m/s and 0.01 rad/s upwards. 0.005 m/s and 0.02 rad/s below are the twist bins'
edges and the commissioning's breakaway figures. They are not a hard threshold in this plant's response: the
realistic profile has no explicit base breakaway, and the 0.002 m/s step still moves at 0.0019 m/s, while the
0.01 rad/s step turns at 0.0003 rad/s (Task 4 review F4). B5's output dead zone of 0.005 m/s and 0.01 rad/s is a
controller transformation, separate from the plant.

### Finding 1: the depth ratchet is B5's output stage, not the plant

In every cycle of the three ratchet episodes (E1's window: from the deepest pair 5 mm inside its margin to the
contact), the command B5 sent turns the base the other way from the first control of B5's own plan:

| Run | Cycles | Plan's yaw rate (median) | Sent yaw rate (median) | Depth change per 0.1 s: plan's control | Sent command | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| door_105_mission F2 s20 | 341 | -0.026 rad/s | +0.054 rad/s | -0.88 mm | +2.05 mm | +1.68 mm |
| door_then_beam F2 s5-n | 501 | +0.026 | -0.054 | -0.86 | +1.91 | +1.15 |
| store_room F2 abs s18 | 831 | +0.026 | -0.054 | -0.72 | +1.49 | +0.69 |

The depth changes in the table are one-cycle rollouts of B5's own model from B5's state, projected on the pair's
distance gradient at that state, summed and scaled to 0.1 s. "Measured" is the B5 state's actual change, projected
the same way. Its sum matches the logged depth change: 114.3 mm against 114.1 mm over door_105's episode. All
windows are smooth (no kink). The base's commanded forward speed is 0 throughout: the base turns in place.

The plan's control predicts the pair leaving (-0.7 to -0.9 mm per 0.1 s, as E1 found from the plans' stage-1 slack).
B5's own model, fed the sent command, predicts it going in faster than it does (+1.5 to +2.1 mm). The plant then
executes less of that turn than the model assumes (Finding 2), which slows the drift but does not cause it.

**The mechanism** (`controller.cpp`, the command path of an `ok` cycle):

1. The plan's first control is clamped to the rate band around the command sent last: +-0.016 rad/s per 20 ms
   cycle, from `yaw_accel_radps2` 0.8.
2. `breakaway_output` then raises a command below the breakaway to the breakaway, 0.07 rad/s (the envelope's
   `breakaway_yaw_radps`, Unity's value), in the clamped command's sign, whenever the base reads "at rest" (measured
   |omega| below `rest_yaw_radps`, 0.02).

Once one kick has gone out at +0.07, a plan asking for -0.026 is clamped to +0.054. If the base reads at rest, the
command is boosted back to +0.07, the old sign. The sent yaw rates take exactly three levels in door_105's episode:
0.070 (127 cycles), 0.054 (127) and 0.038 (87), a kick and its rate-limited decay. The plant's measured yaw rate
alternates between about 0.026 and 0.002 rad/s, so the base reads "at rest" every few cycles and the kick repeats.
Under that schedule of repeated rest and boost, the command does not cross zero. Without the resets, the
rate-limited command would decay through zero. The rest test also needs the forward speed below 0.01 m/s: these
episodes turn in place, so the yaw-only account applies to them.

The status is `ok` and the gate passes in every cycle (absolute rule). These plans pass the configured gate and
locally predict a decreasing depth; that does not certify their swept clearance. This is B5 logic. It does not depend on this plant except through how often the base reads at rest. Whether Unity shows it is
not measured (E5).

E1's two candidates were (a) B5's base model against the wheel-contact base and (b) sub-breakaway commands the plant
does not execute as B5 assumes. The data supports (b) in a sharper form: B5's own output stage turns a small planned
correction into a kick the other way.

### Finding 2: the plant's base against B5's model

The one-stage (0.1 s) error of B5's model with the command B5 sent, over every 0.1 s window of the 18 runs
(`rollout.md`, `model_rollout.py report`). The "twist" rows feed the measured twist instead of the command:

| Horizon | Input | forward p50/p90/max (mm) | lateral p50/p90/max (mm) | yaw p50/p90/max (mrad) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 s | command | 0.08 / 0.63 / 19.4 | 0.00 / 0.49 / 10.5 | 0.00 / 2.64 / 20.3 |
| 0.1 s | measured twist | 0.00 / 0.19 / 2.2 | 0.00 / 0.51 / 10.3 | 0.00 / 0.37 / 4.6 |

The yaw and forward errors are command tracking: the measured twist removes 86 % of the yaw p90. The lateral error is
kinematic: no twist input removes it, because the unicycle has no lateral motion.

**Turn response.** The yaw rate the base achieves, as a share of the command:

| abs omega (rad/s) | below 0.02 | 0.02-0.07 | 0.07-0.2 | 0.2-0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| open loop, in place | 0.04 | 0.50 | 0.72 | 0.90 |
| closed loop, 0.1-0.3 m/s | 0.10 | 0.51 | 0.85 | 0.95 |

The open-loop ratios are from the excitation, which turns in place. The closed-loop ratios are from the 18 runs at
0.1-0.3 m/s. The understeer grows sharply as the yaw rate falls below about 0.07 rad/s. The ratchet episodes command
0.038-0.07 rad/s, where the base achieves about half.

**Delays and lags** (alignment fits, delays to +-0.01 s; closed-loop unless stated):

| Channel | Delay | Time constant | Gain | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v | 0.000 s | 0.030 s | 0.99 | open loop 0.035 / 0.235 s: the plant's acceleration ramp on steps, which B5's own limits avoid |
| omega | 0.010 s | 0.000 s | 0.87 | the gain is the turn response above, nonlinear in omega |
| arm, pipeline (command to joint) | 0.005-0.045 s | 0-0.04 s | 0.98-1.01 | delay + time constant 0.035-0.045 s on every joint |
| arm, servo (reference to joint) | 0.030-0.035 s | 0 | 0.99-1.00 | the plant's 30 ms arm command delay |

The arm's servo fit leaves out 18243 intervals as `equal_states` (B5's and the measured arm joints equal) and 818 as
`at_limit` (a reference at the 0.6 rad/s limit, a possible clip). The equal-state records are mostly quiet: 97 % have
a reference velocity of exactly 0, and the requested joint velocities are tiny. Their provenance is not resolved,
though (Task 4 review F3). `ArmCommandPath::b5_state` returns the measured joints both without a sent trajectory and
for a reference more than 0.15 rad away, and a displaced stationary reference is quiet too. They stay excluded as
ambiguous.

The servo fit is read on what it keeps: 53466 intervals, of which 19620 carry a reference velocity above 0.01 rad/s
on some joint. A producer flag would only be needed to interpret the excluded ones.

**Sideslip.** Against the executed twist (the lateral dynamics alone), the best pooled first-order fit has no
build-up in either data set. The coefficients disagree fourfold. Each fit's sensitivity is given as the rms with the
time constant fixed (delay and gain refitted), against the rms of no lateral model at all (Task 4 review F2):

| Data | Transitions | Coefficient | Best tau / delay | rms at tau 0 / 0.1 / 0.3 s (m/s) | rms, no model | Variance explained |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open-loop excitation | 12 | 0.049 | 0.000 / 0.020 s | 0.006743 / 0.006772 / 0.006782 | 0.006880 | 4 % |
| the 18 closed-loop runs | 167 | 0.200 | 0.000 / 0.005 s | 0.004426 / 0.004519 / 0.004668 | 0.005499 | 35 % |
| door_then_beam s5-n alone | 11 | -0.123 | 0.195 / 0.200 s (bound) | 0.004425 / 0.004407 / 0.004407 | 0.004464 | 2 % |

The coefficient is lateral m/s per v x omega.

- **The closed-loop runs** prefer no build-up: fixing tau at 0.1 s costs 2.1 % rms and at 0.3 s 5.5 %, and the fit
  explains 35 % of the lateral speed's variance.
- **The open-loop excitation** does not identify the time constant. The profile is flat (0.4 % at 0.1 s), and the
  fit explains 4 %: its lateral speed is mostly something other than v x omega.
- **door_then_beam s5-n's** per-run optimum (tau 0.195 s) has a negative coefficient, a delay on the search's bound,
  explains 2 %, and its profile is flat. It is unidentified, not evidence of a slow lateral response.

Against the command, the open-loop delay is 0.155 s, which is the base's own lag.

**Provisional ruling: no lateral state.** The only data that identify the time constant put it at 0 and penalise
0.1 s, but a slow component of small size is not excluded. Task 6 re-tests it on held-out data.

The closed-loop coefficient is the one B5's commands meet. The difference is not explained here: arm poses and loads,
turn histories, and chatter in the finite-difference lateral speed are all candidates. Task 6's identification fits it
per pose and on both data sets.

- **The depth ratchet:** the three contact runs whose pair drifts in at rest are the latched ones. door_then_beam
  s15-n, the panel on the low beam, has no latch. It is E1's linearization case, handed to E4.
- **gate_right_swing, finding (c):** three of the four failing seeds latch, in 87 to 508 cycles; s7 does not, though
  it is boosted in 122 cycles. The passing seeds never go 5 mm deep and never latch. The latch is therefore a
  candidate for the failing swings, not yet their shown cause.
The latched counts flag a sign mismatch at a kick level. An ordinary rate-limited reversal can also meet that
definition without a rest-triggered loop, so outside the three episodes analysed above they are an indicator, not a
diagnosis.

- **store_room for B5 alone:** no latched cycles and at most 4 boosted ones per run (1, 0, 0, 4 and 2). The base turns into the 1.05 m door at full speed (0.3 m/s,
  0.2-0.4 rad/s), with the plans seeing no violation. A pair the trace had not carried before (5|3, the door S frame)
  then appears 5-7 mm deep within about 0.2 s. The gate holds, and the braking carries the pair to 12-27 mm (seeds 1
  and 3). Whether that jump is the turn response (understeer, sideslip) or the pair entering the collision rows late
  (the row budget, linearization) is not told apart by this measurement.

## The output stage follows the plan (Task 4b; spec Amendment 1)

**Change.** Switch `breakaway_follows_plan` (`wholebody_mpc.yaml`, false until judged). From rest the output stage
lifts a base component to its breakaway only when it has the sign of the plan's component and the plan's component is
outside its dead zone. A component the rate band leaves opposing the plan goes out as clamped, decaying at the rate
limit; it is not re-kicked. The frozen stage is unchanged when the switch is off.

Tests (`test_controller.cpp`):

- `BreakawayOutput.FollowPlanNeverBoostsAgainstThePlan`: the rule, per component.
- `BreakawayOutput.FollowPlanEndsTheLatchMeasuredOnThePlant`: the output path cycle by cycle with the measured latch
  inputs. The frozen stage re-kicks against the plan for good; the new one never grows against the plan and turns the
  plan's way within 7 cycles.

Suites: mpc 422, medium 111, campaign 104 (the equivalence suite included), 0 failures.

## Identification (stage 2, Task 6; 2026-11-12; refreshed after the Task 6 review)

`tools/plant/identify_tracking.py`; runs `e2_identify.sh` into `experiment_runs/e2/ident/`. The first pass
(`experiment_runs/e2/ident_v1/`) had the defects of the Task 6 review (F1-F5); every number below is from the
corrected tool.

**Method.** The fit works per 20 ms interval on the measured body speeds (the time convention):

- for each turn scale c on a grid (0.005-0.1 rad/s), the turn gains are the least-squares solution inside the
  loader's ranges: a fixed gain is subtracted first, and an active bound refits the other gain;
- the speed falling in turns and the sideslip are then single-coefficient regressions;
- the arm delay is the pipeline alignment's delay plus its lag.

Closed-loop runs are grouped by physical case (`<cell>-<kind>-s<seed>`). An identical run under another config is
dropped, and each case's fit or held-out role follows a stable hash, so it is the same at every grip. Each fit
records its input runs, their roles and the plant profile its logs name (a manifest in `fit_*.json`). The
excitation's responses are saved (`excitation_records.json.gz`), and a refit from them reproduces the file byte for
byte.

This is an effective map for the sampled command histories, not isolated physical constants. The interval objective
weights transients differently from the 0.1 s rollout. The base's short lag (about 0.03 s) and delay (about 0.01 s)
shift the static fit of g_0 and c, as the reviewer's synthetic check shows. **g_0 is weakly identified:** the
training error barely changes between g_0 0.025 and 0.14 (the review measured 1.0417 against 1.0415 mrad), and the
two splits of the same runs gave those two values. The online correction (Amendment 2) adapts it.

## The online correction (Task 6b; spec Amendment 2, narrowed after the Task 6b review)

`TrackingEstimator` (`mobile_manipulator_mpc`) corrects g_inf and the sideslip k from the base's measured motion. It
is a Kalman filter with random-walk states, updated per 0.1 s window. g_0, c and the speed term stay at the
identified values.

**The review found C1.** The first version improved the one-stage prediction at every grip, but over B5's horizon it
was worse than the fixed prior at mu 0.6. Ramps at the start of motion and slow in-place turns (stiction) pulled g_inf
down: the static map cannot explain them. g_0 and k wandered to their bounds.

**The revised version learns nothing from these windows:**

- a ramp (the commanded yaw rate spans more than 0.03 rad/s);
- the first 0.3 s of a steady command (the lag tail);
- for the turn gain, turns below 0.08 rad/s;
- for k, weak excitation (|mean v x omega| below 0.005);
- an implausible innovation (normalized innovation squared above 9);
- a base that did not turn when commanded (blocked wheels).

The initial covariance is 0.1 of the cap. Tests: 17 on a synthetic base, among them the start of motion with a lagging
base, in-place stiction, blocked wheels, the heading wrap, a bad step mid-window, and the covariance cap. Two of the
reviewer's mutants (no yaw wrap, no window restart) now fail tests.

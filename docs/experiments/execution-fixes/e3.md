What the tool reports, and what to check on the ensembles before reading anything into it:

- `e_track` is measured from B5's state, so it holds the arm's offset between its streamed reference and its measured
  joints at the planning cycle; `e_state` is that offset alone. In this run the two are of the same size at 0.1 s
  while the arm moves (about 9 to 10 mm at the 95th percentile on the arm rows): the short-lookahead error may be
  mostly the standing offset, not an error that builds up over the lookahead.
- `e_lin` at the traced stages is at most 0.2 mm at the 95th percentile: the solved plan is a small step from its
  nominal. This is the error at the stages; the error between stages is E4's and is not in the trace.
- `e_total` grows with the lookahead much faster than `e_track` (panel, arm regime: 12.9 mm at 0.1 s, 49.1 mm at
  1.0 s): it also holds B5 changing its plan at later cycles, which is not a tracking error.
- The run has no arm or panel row in the `straight` regime: under this beam the arm moves throughout. The tightness
  cells' straight approaches (Task 3) are what supplies that regime.

**What the two modes compare.** The realistic runs are the MuJoCo plant with B5 on the identified model and its gain
correction; the ideal runs are a plant that executes the commands as sent with B5 on the unicycle. The modes differ
in the plant and in B5's prediction model. A difference between them is a difference of two plant-and-model packages;
it does not isolate tracking, in either direction (Task 4 review C3). A matched control would hold B5's model policy
fixed across the plants; none was run.

**Validity of the runs.**

- Campaign runs do not plan against the wall clock. Every run's Hello has `iteration_budget: 30`; the session then
  sets the medium planner's solve deadline to 10 s and delivers each plan at the profile's fixed latency of 0.28 s.
  The largest medium solve in the sweep is 0.63 s. `medium_capped` counts cycles that used up the iteration budget.
  The host's load, the battery and the trace overhead therefore do not reach the plans through a deadline (Task 4
  review I1; the first version of this section said the opposite).
- The realistic leg ran 15:56 to 17:50 with no sleep in the power log, part of it on battery.
- The ideal leg: the host slept from 17:54:31 to 17:56:08 (lid closed, then a thermal emergency sleep). Four i050
  runs were in flight (`e3_beam_straight_3` seed 19, `e3_beam_straight_4` seeds 4, 5, 6). They are kept in
  `experiment_runs/e3/sweep_slept` and were run again under the unchanged condition. The reruns' `run.mmlog` files
  are byte-identical to the originals (review check), as the fixed budget predicts: the sleep changed nothing.
- The pipeline check on the ideal plant: over the 320 i050 runs, the per-run 95th percentile of |e_track| on the arm
  and panel rows is 0.000 mm at 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 s, and the largest single value is 0.001 mm (limit: 1 mm). This
  checks the analysis, not the comparison of the modes.
- All 128 groups (cell, mode, margin) have 20 summaries with a clearance and a contact count.

What the tables say:

- **Passage does not improve monotonically as the margin shrinks, but some cells do improve.** The beam passes about
  9 in 10 at every margin. The realistic gap passes 86, 106, 106 and 97 of 160. The gain from 0.05 to 0.04 m is
  carried by single cells, most of all `e3_gap_turn_2`: 9 of 20 at 0.05 m and 19 of 20 at 0.04 m, ten seeds reaching
  only at 0.04 m and none only at 0.05 m (one-sided exact sign test p = 0.001, exploratory, uncorrected), and 0.04 m
  is safe there in all 20 runs (smallest clearance 31.6 mm) while 0.05 m is not. Over the eight realistic gap cells,
  34 cell-seeds reach only at 0.04 m and 14 only at 0.05 m. Below 0.04 m the same cell falls back (13 of 20 at
  0.03 m). The sweep changes B5's margin and the medium planner's together, so it does not say which one opens the
  cell. The data neither show a benefit of a learned margin nor rule one out.
- **Counting safe passages, 0.04 m is the best fixed margin on the gap and 0.05 m on the beam** (realistic, safe and
  reached: gap 86, 105, 104, 1; beam 154, 144, 54, 39 from 0.05 to 0.02 m). At 0.02 m almost every passage is
  unsafe.
- **The runs that do not reach end near the obstacle.** Of the 666, 482 stalled (45 s without progress) and 184
  timed out; 658 ended within 1 m of the obstacle's centre along the approach (review check). In them the medium
  planner is infeasible in 69 % of its cycles (median) and uses up its iteration budget in 77 %, against 1 % and
  23 % in the runs that reach. This locates the failures; it does not give their cause. The analysis that would: a
  replay of the first persistently infeasible planning state with the four margins and a larger iteration budget.
- **Clearance follows the margin.** On the gap, the median clearance of the runs that reach falls by 10 mm per
  0.01 m of margin in both modes (ideal 46.5, 36.4, 26.1, 16.0 mm).
- **The realistic package keeps a few millimetres less.** Its median is 2 to 3 mm below the ideal package's on the
  gap and 2 to 9 mm on the beam; the 5th percentile 4 to 10 mm below. With the caveat above, this is not a measure
  of tracking alone.
- **No fixed margin is safe on every cell** by the definition (every run counted). At 0.05 m, four realistic gap
  cells fail it through 7 runs that did not reach: they stayed near the obstacle on plans with slack (for example
  `e3_gap_turn_3` seed 16: 10.0 mm, 7 executed slack plans). These are real safety failures and stay in the verdict.
  The ideal package has such runs too (`e3_gap_straight_4` at 0.05 m, seed 19: a contact, -23.9 mm, 31 executed
  slack plans): mechanisms other than tracking can produce them, which does not show that tracking has no part in
  the realistic ones.

**CP1's Trade criterion, both branches** (spec Amendment 1: on an informative cell, the realistic plant needs a
larger safe fixed margin, OR passes significantly fewer). Scored here as written; whether it is the right gate, given
what the modes compare, is a ruling for CP1.

- *Larger safe margin: met.* The smallest safe margin is one step larger on the realistic package on 9 of the 14
  cells that have one in both modes. Four of the nine are informative in both modes: `e3_beam_turn_1`, `_2`, `_3`
  (0.05 against 0.04 m) and `e3_gap_straight_1` (0.04 against 0.03 m).
- *Significantly fewer passages: no deficit detected.* No cell and margin has a one-sided Fisher p below 0.05 (64
  tests, uncorrected; the smallest 0.064 at `e3_gap_turn_2`, 0.02 m, where the counts are 13 against 18 of 20). The
  same-seed paired test gives none either (review: smallest 0.0625). This is not equivalence: a 25-point deficit is
  not excluded at that cell. Pooled over the gap the realistic package passes more (395 against 321 of 640).
- *Informative cells* by the rule fixed before the sweep: 9 of 16 realistic, 10 of 16 ideal. The rule is an
  eligibility screen: a flat pass rate counts as informative, and it excludes `e3_gap_turn_2` (realistic) for its
  fall at 0.03 m. The flag is not evidence of a threshold.

## CP1: go or no-go (Task 5, 2026-11-16)

The question: do the measurements justify building the learned margin (Stage 2)? The criteria are spec Amendment 1's;
all four for a go. The ruling is the user's. Tables: [e3_cp1_m050.md](e3_cp1_m050.md) and
[e3_cp1_m030.md](e3_cp1_m030.md) (generated), and the sweep's [e3_sweep.md](e3_sweep.md).

**How the numbers are made.** The realistic 0.05 m leg (320 runs, 240 reached) and, as a repeat, the 0.03 m leg (320
runs, 251 reached). Rows against the cell's obstacle only (arm and panel rows; these cells give the base no row
against the obstacle). Each number is a run's 95th percentile over its rows, then the median and range over runs;
runs with fewer than 20 rows in a group are left out. "Normal passages" are the runs that reached. e_track is
measured from B5's state (the measured base, the arm's streamed reference), so it holds the arm's standing
reference-to-measured offset; the same error from the true state at the planning cycle is computed on the same rows
(`trace_records(..., start="both")`).

At least 5 mm on the panel rows at every lookahead and on the arm rows from 0.5 s. m030 repeats it (panel 7.0 to 10.9 mm, arm 4.4 to 10.6 mm). | met |

By the rule (all four for a go) this is a **no-go**: Predictability is not met, and it is the criterion that carries
the question after the slow control was dropped. Dependence stays unavailable; it was not scored as failed.

**Why a state-dependent margin has little to work with here.**

- Near the obstacle the motion is of one kind. In the normal passages 98 to 99 % of the obstacle's rows are in the
  arm, turning, turning+arm and other regimes, and their errors are of the same size: per-run p95 at 0.5 s, median
  over runs, 7.0 (arm), 7.9 (turning+arm), 4.3 (turning) and 2.7 mm (other) on the arm rows; 7.4, 9.2, 5.8 and 4.4 mm
  on the panel rows. The near-zero error of the `straight` regime in the pooled table of Task 4 belongs to runs
  standing before the obstacle, not to a passage.
- A margin that follows the commands therefore sits near the constant bound. The linear fit comes out above it
  (a negative saving) in most groups; the binned check shows the form is not what limits it.
- History adds nothing: the held-out gain of the commands of the previous 0.5 s is -0 to 5 % (m050) and -3 to 6 %
  (m030); 10 % was the bar for proposing approach C.

**The arm's standing offset.** From the true state the same rows give a per-run p95 of 1.6 mm (arm) and 1.4 mm
(panel) at 0.1 s, against 4.1 and 6.7 mm from B5's state: the offset is 61 % and 79 % of the error there. At 0.5 s it
is 18 % (6.6 against 8.0 mm; 6.2 against 7.6 mm), and at 1.0 s 12 % on the arm rows and none on the panel rows. So an
arm-lag term in B5's model (as E2 did for the base) would take most of the short-lookahead error, where B5's next
commands are decided, and little of the error at 0.5 s and beyond.

**The other numbers the plan asks for.**

- *Lookahead:* the shortest at which Size holds is 0.1 s on the panel rows and 0.5 s on the arm rows. At 0.1 s the
  error is mostly the standing offset.
- *Rows* (decision 3): arm and panel rows qualify by Size. The base has no row against these obstacles, so the cells
  give no evidence for or against a margin on the base rows.
- *Linearization* (E4): per-run p95 e_lin is 0.0 mm (median over runs; at most 3.9 mm in a run) at 0.1 and 0.2 s on
  both row types, against an e_total of 4.5 to 9.2 mm. Linearization is not what the margin is spent on here.
- *Best fixed margin:* none is safe on every cell with every run counted. Counting passages that are also safe:
  0.04 m on the gap (105 of 160 against 86 at 0.05 m) and 0.05 m on the beam (154 against 144). *Floor candidate*
  (the smallest margin safe on every straight cell): none (`e3_gap_straight_4` is safe at no margin); without that
  cell, 0.05 m (`e3_beam_straight_1` is unsafe below it).
- *Rows left out:* about 11 % of the traced rows are of plans B5 did not execute (118,217 per stage in m050); 960
  first-frame rows and 1,063 to 12,666 rows past the log's end per stage. The tables describe executed plans.

**Findings against the premise.**

- Passage on these cells is not set by the margin in general. The runs that fail stall near the obstacle with the
  medium planner infeasible in 69 % of its cycles (Task 4). One cell opens at 0.04 m (`e3_gap_turn_2`, 9 to 19 of
  20); whether B5's margin or the medium planner's does it is unknown.
- Slower is not easier: at a slower reference the gap stops passing (slow-control pilot).
- The unsafe runs at 0.05 m are stalled runs on slack plans, on the ideal package too.

**What the evidence points to instead** (each is a proposal, none is measured):

1. An arm-lag (standing offset) term in B5's model: removes 61 to 79 % of the 0.1 s error on the arm and panel rows.
2. The medium planner's feasibility in the passage: a replay of the first persistently infeasible planning state
   over the four margins and a larger iteration budget (Task 4 review I1) would say whether the margin, the search
   budget or the geometry stops it. This is what limits passage on these cells.
3. A fixed 0.04 m arm and panel margin: more safe passages on the gap, fewer on the beam; not a clear gain.
4. The stalled runs on slack plans: a safety problem at every margin, in both packages.

**Limits.** One site, two obstacle shapes, the plant not Unity. The realistic-against-ideal comparison is not a
matched control (a matched one was not run). Predictability is judged on a first fit with three features; a richer
model could do better, though the binned check and the regimes' similar errors make 30 % unlikely on these cells.
Irregular passages (the campaign revision) may differ and are owed a rerun whatever the ruling.

**Ruling:** open (the user's).
